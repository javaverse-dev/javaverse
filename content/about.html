Javaverse is my notebook for the parts of software engineering that do not fit into a quick answer. The questions that only show up after the tutorial ends: why a framework behaves the way it does, what a design decision costs two years later, and what actually happens when clean code meets real traffic.

Java is home. Quarkus and Spring are the tools I reach for most. But the scope is wider than any single framework: system design, cloud-native architecture, developer productivity, and the fast-moving world of AI-assisted software development all find their way in here.

Who I am

I'm a Software Development Engineer currently working at Siemens. Over the last 11+ years I have designed, built, debugged and maintained software across many layers: REST APIs, microservices, integrations and the plumbing that keeps all of it running.

Those years taught me that the most interesting problems are rarely about syntax. They are about trade-offs, boundaries and the assumptions nobody wrote down. That is the kind of engineering I enjoy most, and the kind I write about.

How I work

I use AI tools openly. They help me explore unfamiliar APIs, prototype quickly, challenge my reasoning and tighten drafts. What they do not do is decide what gets published. I run the code, verify the claims and apply my own experience before anything goes live. If my name is on it, I stand behind it.

Why Javaverse

Our industry is loud. New frameworks, new models and new "this changes everything" claims arrive every week. I would rather pick one idea, build something with it, break it, and come back with an honest answer about what is really there. Fewer topics, deeper understanding, working code.
